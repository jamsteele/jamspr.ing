# 📚 The Library — Planning Doc

> *"A little illustrated room you can poke around in. Some things make you smile, some open into stories, some just exist to be found."*

**Status:** Concept locked, MVP scoped, scaffold built.
**URL:** `jamspr.ing/library`
**Last updated:** May 9, 2026

---

## 🌟 The One-Sentence Pitch

A cozy, illustrated web room in the home/office of a 25-year-old Vancouver-based world traveler — they've just stepped out, and you can poke around the desk, shelves, and trinkets they've collected on their travels. Some objects open into hand-curated public-domain short stories, each tied to the place it came from.

---

## 🎒 The Character (Inhabitant)

- **Age:** 25
- **Home base:** Vancouver, BC
- **She / her** — the inhabitant is a young woman (informs object choices: hairpin, fan, jewelry box, etc.)
- **Vibe:** World traveler / writer / quiet adventurer / aesthete-on-a-budget
- **Has been collecting books and stories from her travels for ~7 years** (since gap-year days)
- **Trinkets are souvenirs, not props** — every object on display is something she actually picked up on a trip and kept as a memory. The story link is a quiet reward for clickers; the object should also pass as normal aesthetic-traveler decor.
- **Frame:** "She just stepped out" — half-finished tea, journal open on the desk, cat warmth where she sat
- **Never seen, never named** — the room tells you who she is
- **Optional small detail:** a notebook or postcard in the room with initials + a place + a year (e.g. *"M.K. — Lisbon, 2024"*) for visitors who notice

## 🏡 The Room — Aesthetic & Feeling

**Reference universe:** Studio Ghibli warmth (light, palette, plants, food, weather), but the visual identity is its own thing — *not* a Ghibli room. Think Whisper of the Heart's writing nook, Tintin's office, Wes Anderson eclecticism, the Calvin Trillin / Frances Mayes "writer-traveler" register.

**Texture:** Bohemian-aesthete-on-a-budget. NOT Indiana Jones or expensive antiques. Cheap-pretty things from markets, polaroids on the wall, train tickets stuck in books, mismatched mugs.

**Light:** Soft, warm, golden-hour-ish. Maybe slightly different by time of day (Phase 2).

### Vancouver-Coded Touches (subtle, not branded)

- 🌧️ **Window view:** Rain on glass, mountains in the distance — **The Lions** visible in silhouette (echoes Pauline Johnson's *The Two Sisters*)
- 🥾 **Pair of muddy hiking boots** by the door (Blundstones or worn leather)
- 🌿 **Cedar branches** in a glass jar
- 🪴 **Jade plant** on the windowsill
- 🍒 **Cherry blossom sprig** in a small vase (callback to jamspr.ing energy + real Vancouver spring)
- 🧥 **Rain jacket** draped over the chair back
- 🐋 **Small carved wooden orca** or salmon on the desk
- 🗺️ **A trail map** of the North Shore mountains pinned to the wall
- ☕ **A steaming mug** of something hot (still has steam if MVP can pull it off)
- 🧶 **Crochet blanket** in West Coast colors (sage, slate, dusty pink) draped on the chair
- 🎵 **Record player** in the corner (Phase 2: clickable, plays a few seconds of something)

These should feel **environmental**, not branded. The room signals Pacific NW without screaming "I LIVE IN VANCOUVER."

Every clickable object that opens a story is a souvenir from the place that story is from. This is the core mechanic.

|-------|------------------|----------------|
| The Two Sisters | Carved cedar piece OR small framed photo of The Lions | Wall / shelf |
| Boy Who Drew Cats | Origami cat | Desk |
| Open Window | Small framed misty-marsh watercolor | Wall |
| Nightingale and the Rose | Pressed rose under glass | Bookshelf |
| Tell-Tale Heart | Old brass clock (still ticking) | Mantel / shelf |
| Postmaster | Glass ink bottle with a quill | Desk |

| The Necklace | Costume jewelry in a velvet box | Vanity / dresser |

## 🎯 MVP (Phase 1)

**Goal:** Ship in 1–2 weekends. Prove the concept. Make something genuinely shareable.

### Scope

- **One static room scene**, single POV (no panning around)
- **8 clickable objects total:**
  - 6 books/trinkets that open stories (the strongest 6 below)
  - 2 ambient/flavor objects (cat that purrs, mug that steams) — non-story interactions
- **6 stories ready to read** (curated MVP set, geographically diverse)
- **Reader UX:** click trinket → modal pops with the story → close button → back to room
- **Mobile responsive** — tap-friendly hotspots, readable text
- **No accounts, no notes, no backend logic needed** — pure static frontend
- **Hosted as a route on jamspr.ing** (Railway, Express server already running)

A short tour: Vancouver, Japan, England, USA, Russia, Canada-comedy.

### MVP Easter Egg (1)

Pick **one** hidden interaction. Suggestion: clicking the cherry blossom sprig three times causes petals to drift across the whole room briefly. (Callback to jamspr.ing.)

### MVP Tech Stack (Recommended)

- **HTML/CSS/JavaScript**, no framework (the existing jamspr.ing project doesn't use one — match that)
- **Stories as Markdown files** in `public/library/stories/` (simple to author, ~10–30 KB each)
- **Story metadata as a single `index.json`** so the frontend knows what stories exist, their countries, and reading times — without loading them all upfront
- **Room as one large illustrated image** with invisible-div hotspots layered over it
- **CSS animations** for ambient stuff (steam, leaves, gentle wind) — no heavy JS
- **One easter egg** with a tiny canvas or CSS overlay
- **Hosted:** As a path on existing jamspr.ing Railway deployment (see Hosting & Integration below)
- **URL:** `jamspr.ing/library` ✅

#### Trinkets that must look right (recognizability matters)

A trinket has to be recognizable enough that visitors who *know the source* spot the link, but generic enough to pass as everyday traveler decor for those who don't.

- **The Lions / Two Sisters trinket** — a small framed picture of *The Lions*, the twin peaks above Vancouver. People here know this skyline. Render it accurately or it'll feel off.
  - **West Lion (left when viewed from Vancouver):** taller (1,654 m), sharp pointed summit
  - **East Lion (right):** shorter (1,606 m), more rounded / blockier top
  - **Saddle:** a clear notch/dip between them — they read as twins, not a single ridge
  - **Look:** snowcapped most of the year, dark hornblende-diorite faces, often photographed from the south (Stanley Park / English Bay) with the city or water in foreground
  - **Indigenous name:** Ch'ích'iyúy Elx̱wíḵn (Twin Sisters) — which IS the story
  - **Reference photo (public domain, 1919, Leonard Frank):** https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/1e/The_Lions_of_the_North_Shore_mountains.jpg
  - **Treatment idea:** her own Instax polaroid taken from Stanley Park, clipped to the polaroid string with a mini wooden peg

#### The wall — string of polaroids on warm-white fairy lights

The "she's been everywhere" signal comes from a **string of polaroid photos hung on warm-white copper-wire LED fairy lights**, mini wooden clothespin clips, draped along one wall of the room. This is the iconic late-2010s/2020s aesthete-traveler photo wall — modern, lived-in, and it doubles as the room's primary soft light source.

**Style spec:**
- **Lights:** warm-white (not multicolor, not bulb-style), thin copper wire, ~20–30 lights along the string. Avoid Christmas/café/festival energy.
- **Polaroids:** Instax-style square instant photos with the white border, slightly imperfect spacing, some tilted, a couple overlapping. Faded color, light leaks, slight grain — they should feel like real prints, not stock images.
- **Captions:** a few have her handwriting on the white border ("Kyoto, 2024", "Paris, 2022")
- **Mix portrait + landscape orientations** — uniform = stiff

**Recognizable places to photograph (8 polaroids; 1 per story-country):**

1. **The Lions, Vancouver** — anchor shot, larger or center-stage; her home (see brief above for accurate rendering)
2. **Eiffel Tower, Paris** — for Maupassant's Necklace
3. **Fushimi Inari torii gates, Kyoto** — for Miyazawa's Restaurant of Many Orders. The orange tunnel is unmistakable.
4. **Taj Mahal, Agra** — for Tagore's Postmaster
5. **Cliffs of Moher, Ireland** — for Wilde's Nightingale and the Rose
6. **St. Basil's Cathedral, Moscow** — for Chekhov & Tolstoy. Onion domes = instant Russia.
7. **Brooklyn Bridge, NYC at dusk** — for Poe's Tell-Tale Heart. Better than a generic skyline.
8. **Banaue rice terraces, Philippines** — for Sagacious Marcela

Skipped (no recognizable landmark, story carried by trinket alone): England (Saki), Ontario (Leacock), Yukon (Service), PEI (Montgomery).

**Why this also serves the MVP requirements:**
- **Solves the room's light source.** The plan calls for "soft, warm, golden-hour-ish" — fairy lights deliver this directly.
- **Doubles as the ambient animation.** A subtle twinkle on one or two lights (CSS animation) satisfies the "ambient animation" MVP requirement without inventing steam/rain/leaves.
- **Easter egg candidate.** Clicking the cherry blossom sprig could trigger a soft wave of brightening across the fairy lights (callback to jamspr.ing).

#### Inhabitant style anchor

When in doubt about *any* room object: **"good taste, but still fun."** She's a 25-year-old who reads, travels, and has a curated visual sensibility — but she's not 40, she's not precious about her things, and her room has fairy lights and a record player, not antiques. If a proposed object reads as "antique shop" or "estate sale," it's wrong. If it reads as "Pinterest 2023" or "thrifted from a market" or "kept from a trip" — it's right.

## 🛠️ Tech Notes / Decisions

**Recommended for MVP:**
- Plain HTML/CSS/JS — match the existing jamspr.ing setup (no framework)
- Stories in `public/library/stories/[slug].md`
- Story metadata in `public/library/stories/index.json` (titles, authors, countries, mins, trinket IDs)
- Hotspots = absolutely positioned `<div>`s over the room image with `data-story` attributes
- Reader = a single modal component, populated with story content on click via `fetch()`
- A tiny markdown parser ([marked](https://github.com/markedjs/marked) ~30 KB) to render story Markdown to HTML — already loaded from CDN in `index.html`
- LocalStorage for read-state in Phase 2 (no accounts ever)

**Don't:**
- Don't add a backend in MVP. The Express server already handles static serving fine — no API routes needed yet.
- Don't over-engineer hotspots with canvas — invisible divs over a static image is fine.
- Don't use a heavy framework (Next.js etc) — overkill for one page, and inconsistent with the rest of jamspr.ing.

## 🌿 Tone & Curation Reminders (For Future-Jill)

- **Middle-school appropriate** = the bar. Spooky, sad, philosophical = fine. Sex, slurs, graphic gore = no.
- **No problematic content.** Every story on the catalog has been vetted. New additions need the same vetting.
- **Curation is the moat.** Don't dilute the shelf with mediocre stories just to grow the catalog. Every piece earns its place.
- **The room is the brand.** Decisions about objects, light, mood should all run through "would the inhabitant actually have this?"
- **Vancouver-coded should stay subtle.** Environmental cues, not branded ones. Mountains in the window, not a Canucks jersey.
- **The visitor is a thoughtful adult.** Optimize for them. Kids reading it is a "no parent will side-eye this" floor, not a target audience.
